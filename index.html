🎪 The Calliope of Possible Futures

Nine pinned wheels. Only three are loaded at a time. Because every wheel is a cylinder, you can see everything that is about to happen — and at each seam, you can see the possible futures and watch which one the machine chooses. Changes are slow and syrupy: a pressure builds underneath until it forces one wheel to slide away and another to slide in.

How to listen to it

It is 15/16, but it is dressed as 4/4. The accents pretend there are four even beats coming, so your ear keeps expecting a normal bar — but the bar is one sixteenth short, so it lands early and the tune is already off again. The chord ring does the same trick bigger: it pretends to resolve at bars 4, 8 and 12 and swerves each time; the real landing is bar 16 rolling straight back into bar 1.

The three wheels

Melody (top) and bass (bottom) are the frame — between them they never play the note that decides major or minor. Harmony (middle) is the lens: it alone supplies that deciding note. Load the Sun lens and the frame sounds major. Let a seam swap in the Shade lens and the same frame, note for note, turns minor. The Re-root lens instead adds the note a third below home, so a C major chord is re-heard as A minor 7 — melody and bass never changed at all.

Seams, swaps and Möbius flips

Each wheel repeats on its own cycle — melody every 4 bars, bass every 3, harmony every 2 — so their seams arrive at different times and the full alignment takes a long while to come round again. At its seam a wheel may stay, swap for one of the other two wheels in its bank (sliding in over a whole cycle while the old one fades), or Möbius-flip: the same wheel, but its feed reverses and it plays its own pattern backwards. The chances next to each wheel are the live possible futures — they move as the boiler pressure moves. At least one wheel always stays, so the machine evolves instead of jumping.
